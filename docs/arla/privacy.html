Arla Privacy Policy

Version 1.7 · Effective October 13, 2026

Cody Dean, publishing as Irishwarhound ("we", "us") makes Arla for Windows and Android. The Arla apps have no account with us, advertising or analytics. Our only server is the licence and update service described under "Your licence and purchase", which also runs the optional relay described under "Your paired devices". Neither ever receives your conversations, files or records in a form we can read. We do not receive automatic usage or crash reports. If you contact us, send a problem report or report an AI reply, we receive what you choose to send.

What stays on your devices

The Windows app keeps its records in %APPDATA%\Arla. It stores settings, projects, goals, tasks, plans, routines, notes and sticky notes, chats, memories, personas, skills, Home layouts, paired-device records, and model-use metadata. Model-use records contain the provider, model, route, timing and token counts, not prompts or replies; there is no automatic size limit yet. Skill-use records keep IDs, times, project and token counts, with the newest 5,000 entries retained. Ordinary error logs rotate at 512 KB with one previous file.

Specialists you make. A specialist that you or Arla create (its name, job, instructions, chosen model and tools) is kept in Arla's settings on that device, like personas; a linked phone shows and changes the computer's list over the device link. The name and job of each specialist you can use in chat go to the AI model with your Arla messages. When Arla uses one, its instructions and Arla's instruction for that task go to the model it runs on: a cloud provider under its own policy, or a local model.

The latest 600 Arla messages form the desktop's live conversation. Older messages are archived in the local memory database before they leave that window; conversation history is kept until you delete it in Knowledge → Memory → Conversation history. You can read and export it there; deletion requires confirmation and makes a local backup first. A paired phone removes deleted Arla messages on its next sync. Long-term memories can be edited or deleted in Memory. Superseded memories unused for 30 days and low-importance, unpinned, never-used memories after 60 days are removed during maintenance; the default limit is 20,000 memories.

Arla indexes readable passages and search vectors from Library folders you add and from each project's configured folder. It skips hidden, ignored, build and unsupported files, backup, signing and secret files, and files over 8 MB. Project folders are reread about every 30 minutes while Arla is idle. Removing a Library folder, or clearing or changing a project's folder, removes its passages on the next read. Arla also reads a project's folder when you ask it to work there and local coding-tool transcripts to show sessions and usage.

Research items you save stay on this computer and are included in its backups. Research uses cloud search only and can keep working in the background while you use the rest of Arla. Every Research assistant question and selected Research and Library excerpts go to a connected cloud model that searches the web itself (Google Gemini with Google Search, or the web search of OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenRouter or Groq), and that service runs the search. If you choose a search service instead, or none of those models can answer, Research sends the search words to that service (Exa, Serper, LangSearch or Tavily) with your key for it, then sends the results and the selected excerpts to the chosen cloud model. These search services receive only the search words, not your conversation, memories or Library. When you use Check this source, the page is fetched through Arla's web lookup path, or, if you turned on Jina Reader, Jina AI fetches that public page for Arla (Arla refuses private addresses and links that carry passwords or keys). Up to 3,000 characters of the page may be included in the request. Research does not use a local model.

Research in the background. A Research job, started in Research or from chat (also from a linked phone), runs on the computer while you do other things:

  • your question goes to the chosen cloud model, which plans the searches and writes the report;
  • the search words go to the search service or searching model;
  • Arla then fetches each source page it found from that public site (up to 6,000 characters per page);
  • excerpts of those pages, up to 14,000 characters in all, go to the chosen cloud model with your question.

Image Studio, on Windows and Android, sends a generation prompt or image search words to the service you select (for example Cloudflare Workers AI, Pixabay or Wikimedia Commons). Search result previews and pictures you explicitly save may be fetched from the image host named by that service. Generated results stay temporary until you press Save; saved pictures, prompts, source links, authors and licence labels stay in the gallery and its backups. Pixabay results are kept on the computer for 24 hours, as Pixabay's rules require. Image service keys use Windows or Android protected storage. The separate Google Images window is ordinary browsing at Google; Arla does not read or automate the page. A right-click Save to Image Studio explicitly downloads one picture to the local gallery.

Meetings. A meeting you record is saved in its project's folder, in a Meetings folder with one folder per meeting holding the transcript, Arla's notes and the recording. On Windows, speech is turned into text on your computer. On Android, Arla asks the phone's speech recognizer to work on the phone only, and Android 13 or newer can keep the recording. Saved meetings are searchable like the rest of the project's folder. A linked phone sends its meetings, recordings included, to the computer over the device link. Deleting a meeting removes its files and search passages on the computer and, when it next connects, on the linked device.

Pictures. When your phone and computer are linked, Image Studio pictures sync between them over the device link. The computer keeps the originals; the phone keeps smaller copies and can fetch a full-size picture. Deleting a picture from both devices removes it on each one when it is reachable. A phone that isn't linked keeps its gallery to itself.

Email and calendar (Windows). If you connect a Google or Microsoft account, Arla keeps on your computer the account address, an optional label you give it (such as Work or Personal), the list of your calendars and which ones you ticked, and a copy of the ticked calendars' events from today through the next 31 days, including each event's location and notes. Event locations and notes are shown to you but are not sent to AI models when Arla lists your events. It refreshes them about every 15 minutes (less often after failures) and shows them in Today, also on your linked phone. Unticking a calendar removes its copied events at the next refresh. The sign-in permission for the account is stored with Windows protected storage and is never included in backups or exports. Arla does not copy your mailbox: see "Email and calendar" below for what it reads and when. Scheduled emails you approve are kept on the computer until they run or you cancel them: the account, recipients, subject, and either the exact text or your instructions for Arla to write it. A linked phone can see each one's one-line description and time and can cancel it, but never receives its text. Email and calendar is an early-access feature: until Google and Microsoft finish reviewing Arla, some accounts may not be able to connect.

Email on Android. You can also connect a Google or Microsoft account on the phone itself (System → Email and calendar), or add one of your computer's accounts to the phone with Add to this phone, which only fills in the address for you to sign in. The phone's sign-in is held by Google Play services or by Microsoft's sign-in library on the phone, never in Arla's own records, backups, exports, sync, the relay or logs, and it never leaves the phone. Arla keeps the account's address, label and when it was connected. The phone's accounts are not sent to your computer. Scheduled emails you approve on the phone are kept on the phone until they run or you cancel them (the same details as on Windows), so they can be sent at the chosen time even when Arla is closed; they are never synced to the computer.

On Android, Arla stores chats, settings, goals, Inbox items and captured photos, notes and sticky notes, offline edits waiting to sync, and downloaded models in the app's storage. It can retain up to 5,000 Arla turns. API keys and the phone-link key are kept in Android protected storage. Windows stores API and phone-link secrets with Windows protected storage; a desktop backup containing those encrypted values cannot make them readable on another PC. Records generally remain until you delete them or the app's data. Removing an app alone may leave its desktop data folder behind.

When information leaves a device

  • AI providers you connect: A request routed to a cloud model sends that provider the current message and bounded context, which can include project instructions, relevant memories, summaries, recent turns, attachments and tool results. When you ask Arla to update records from project files, their extracted text or a summary is sent to your connected cloud Brain. If that model searches the Library, the returned passages and file paths may be sent. Arla does not send the whole chat archive or Library index. A connected cloud provider may be selected automatically for a request; if it fails, Arla may try another connected provider or a local model. A phone request the computer cannot answer may use a phone model or a cloud provider whose key is saved on the phone. Each provider's own policy applies. Optional keyless public models also send the request to their operator. This includes the services on the Connect a service page, such as Z.ai, Cloudflare Workers AI and Cohere. Some services may use what you send to improve their own products, for example Cohere with a trial key and Exa with search words; check a service's policy before sending it personal information.
  • Local models: Ollama on your computer and downloaded phone models process requests locally. Library indexing and memory maintenance use a local model, unless you configured Ollama at another address. When Arla checks that a provider or service works, it asks for its model list or sends a fixed test message, never your conversation.
  • Your paired devices: Projects, plans, tasks, sticky notes, settings, Arla conversation data, pictures and meetings can move directly between your phone and computer over the local network or Tailscale. The current Android app seals phone-link requests, replies and updates with a device key. Older paired phones and the browser phone hub may still use a plain HTTP link with a reusable token on local Wi-Fi; Tailscale encrypts its network traffic. The Devices setting “Refuse phones on the old link” disables that older link.
  • Sync away from home (optional, off by default): if you turn it on, your devices can reach each other through our relay at api.irishwarhound.com when they aren't on the same network. Everything stays sealed with your devices' own key and is wrapped in a second layer of encryption, so the relay passes along data it cannot read and stores none of it. The relay keeps only two random codes that let your devices find each other, whether the relay is on for them, and daily counts used for its limits. Turning it off stops the relay for those devices. It is an early-access feature.
  • Moving API keys: you can send saved API keys from your computer to your phone or back. They go only to your own paired device, sealed over the device link (through the relay too, if it is on, which cannot read them), and only after you accept on the receiving device. Removing keys from the other device also asks there first.
  • Email and calendar (only for accounts you connect, on Windows or Android): Arla reads your email only when you ask it something about your email. It then searches your connected accounts and gets up to 10 matching messages (sender, subject and a short preview), and, when your question needs it, the text of one message, up to 8,000 characters. Attachments are not read. This goes to the chat model you are using to answer you; if that is a cloud model, that provider receives it under its policy. Arla does not keep searched email or message text in its email records or logs, but the conversation keeps what Arla wrote in its reply, like any other chat. Arla writes an email or a reply only when you ask. Before anything is saved or sent at Google or Microsoft, Arla shows a form with the exact account, recipients, subject and text, which you can edit; nothing happens until you press Send now, Save as draft or Schedule in that form. On a linked phone, the same form can appear on the phone, and your answer there counts as it would on the computer. A phone account sends from the phone; a computer account sends from the computer. Adding or changing a calendar event also needs your confirmation in a form. Scheduled emails run at the time you chose, only from the account and to the recipients you approved: with "Send exactly this text" the approved text is sent; with "Arla writes the text", your approved instructions go to the AI model Arla uses at that time (under that provider's policy, as above), and the text it writes is sent without a further check. Arla does not move or delete your email. Disconnecting an account deletes its copied events, its scheduled emails and its sign-in from that device and asks the provider to cancel Arla's access (for a Microsoft account you may also need to remove Arla in your account's app permissions); drafts already saved at your provider stay there.
  • Meeting notes (optional, off by default, Windows): if you turn on "Prepare meeting notes from related email", Arla works on timed meetings in the calendars you ticked, about 15 minutes before each one. It searches your connected accounts for emails from the last 30 days whose subject contains the meeting's title; when the meeting has other attendees, the email must also be from or to one of them. It reads up to 4 of them (up to 6,000 characters each) and sends them, with the meeting's title, time and attendees, to the Brain model Arla is using, which writes a short note. A cloud provider receives them under its own policy. The note is kept with the copied event on the computer and shown before the meeting and in Today, also on your linked phone.
  • Questions about your computer (Windows): when you ask Arla about your computer (for example its hardware, disks, programs, running programs, network or a folder's size) or about a file, Arla looks it up on the computer with fixed read-only tools and sends what it found to the Brain model answering you: file names, sizes and dates, and a file's text when you ask for it. A cloud provider receives this under its own policy. To read a file outside Arla's workspace, Arla first asks your approval for that folder on the computer; you can remove the approval later. Arla never reads secret stores: browser profiles (saved passwords and cookies), Windows Credential Manager, SSH and cloud command-line keys, key and password-database files, or Arla's own sign-in and key files. It does not download cloud-only files to read them. A linked phone can ask these questions through the computer but cannot approve folders. Arla does not scan or index your computer in the background.
  • Changing files on your computer (Windows): when you ask Arla to create, edit, rename, move, copy or delete files, your instruction, the names and paths of the files involved and the parts of their text the change needs go to the AI model Arla uses for the change. Arla uses only models on its list of models that change files reliably (for example GLM-5.3 through NVIDIA's free API); a cloud provider receives this under its own policy, and a paid model is used only after you agree to it. Arla creates and edits files straight away and keeps a copy of each file from before the change, so you can undo it for 30 days (up to 256 MB in all; Undo is skipped if the file has changed again since). Before it overwrites, renames, moves or deletes anything, Arla asks you in the chat and lists the files; deleted files go to the Recycle Bin. The copies stay on the computer and are not included in exported backups, and Arla's record of each change keeps no file contents. A linked phone can ask for changes; they run on the computer. Python: Arla can also run a Python script on your computer with your own Windows permissions. Files a script changes have no Arla copy and no Undo. When Run is set to Ask, Arla shows you the code before it runs. Turning Writing off stops both file changes and Python.
  • Other connections you choose: Fetching a public web page contacts that site; downloading a model contacts its host; OpenRouter phone sign-in contacts OpenRouter; connected MCP clients receive the results of tools they call. A backup exported to a cloud folder is then handled by that service. We do not receive these connections.
  • Voice on Android: Voice input uses the phone's speech recognizer. “Keep speech on this phone” is on by default. If you turn it off, the recognizer may use an online speech service under that service's policy. Arla does not keep an audio recording.

Your licence and purchase

  • The free trial is counted on your computer. The trial's start date and a random install ID that Arla makes for each installed copy (not a hardware fingerprint or anything about you) stay in the app's data folder; the licence key and its signed activation are encrypted with Windows protected storage. None of this is written to logs, crash reports or backups in readable form, and the phone app never receives your licence key.
  • Arla for Android access: the phone keeps its trial start date, the last Google Play purchase state it confirmed and your computer's last licence answer in Android protected storage. This stays on the phone and is not synced or included in backups. A linked phone asks your computer only whether it is licensed; the computer answers yes or no and the kind (bought, beta or trial), never the key, its activation or anything else. The phone unlock is bought through Google Play: Google processes your payment and Google account details under Google's privacy policy, and the app asks Google Play whether your account owns the unlock. We never see your card details, and our server receives nothing about phone purchases. Google gives us an order record in our Google Play developer account (such as the order number, date, product, price and country), which we use only for refunds, accounting and tax.
  • Anonymous trial reports (from Arla 1.0.4): while a copy is in its free trial and online, it tells api.irishwarhound.com when the trial starts and then at most once a day: a random report ID made only for this (not the install ID, so a report can't be linked to a later purchase or licence), the trial's start date, the days it has left and the app version. Nothing about you or your computer is sent. We use the reports only to count trials and to notice copies whose trial runs longer than it should. Our service keeps each report ID with when it was first and last seen and what it last sent, and deletes it 13 months after it was last seen.
  • Licence checks contact our service at api.irishwarhound.com when you enter a key, about every 30 days afterwards while you are online, and when you deactivate a computer. Each request sends only the key or its signed activation, the install ID and the app version.
  • Updates: a licensed copy asks api.irishwarhound.com for updates unless autoUpdate is off, sending its signed activation. A trial copy doesn't check.
  • What our service keeps: for each licence, the purchase's email address, its Stripe checkout reference, when it was created and whether it was refunded or revoked; for each activated computer, its install ID, when it was activated and last checked, and whether it was deactivated. We use this only to deliver, check and support licences (for example to find a lost key or free a lost computer's place) and keep it while the licence exists or as tax and accounting rules require. You can ask us to delete it by email; the licence then stops working.
  • Purchases are processed by Stripe and sold through Link as the seller of record. Stripe collects your payment details, tax information and receipt address under its own privacy policy; we never see your card number. We receive your email address and the purchase record. We email your licence key, and keys you ask to have sent again, through Resend, which handles your email address only to deliver those messages.
  • Beta applications: if you apply to test Arla, our service keeps what you send in the form (your name, email address, how you'd use Arla, your PC and whether you have an Android phone), when you applied, and a one-way code made from your connection's address (only to limit repeated applications; the address itself isn't stored). Each application is also emailed to us. We use it only to choose testers and to contact you about the beta, and delete it when you ask, or within 12 months after the beta programme ends.
  • Downloads and hosting: the website and the download and licence service run on GitHub Pages and Cloudflare, which process your IP address and similar connection data to serve them, under their own policies. We don't use that data to track you. We count installer downloads as a daily total, with nothing about who downloaded.
  • Website visit counts: our website (irishwarhound.com, not the apps) uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits. It records which page was viewed, the site you came from, and your browser type and country, with no cookies or other stored identifiers and no fingerprinting, so it cannot follow you across sites or visits. We see only totals.
  • The waitlist: if you join the waitlist on the Arla web page, our service keeps your email address and when you joined, only to send you one email when Arla can be bought. We send it through Resend and delete your address as soon as it has gone, or straight away if you use the "take me off the list" link or ask us. A one-way code made from your connection's address limits repeated sign-ups (see "Limits on forms").
  • Reviews: if you send a review (from the Arla web page or the app's Help), our service keeps the name you chose to show, your star rating and review, the optional email address you give (never shown; only to contact you about your review, and to check whether you got a free beta licence, which we then mention with your review), whether it came from the website or the app, and when. Each review is emailed to us. Nothing is shown until we approve it; then the name, stars, review and month appear on irishwarhound.com. We remove reviews only for spam, abuse, personal information, fakes or not being about Arla, never for being critical; a removed review is kept with the reason so our moderation can be checked. Ask support@irishwarhound.com and we'll delete your review.
  • Problem reports you send (from Arla 1.0.5): Help → Report a problem builds a report on your computer and shows you the whole text first. Only if you press Send to Irishwarhound is that exact text sent to api.irishwarhound.com: what you wrote, the app version and build, Windows version, processor, memory, graphics, free disk space, licence state (not the key), whether Ollama and voice are ready, the starter pack, recent error times and categories, and, only if you tick them, scrubbed error details and basic settings. Keys, licence identifiers, email and IP addresses, home-folder paths, conversations, records and file contents are removed or never included. We keep a report until we delete it, at most 13 months, and read it only to fix problems. You can instead save the report as a file or email it.
  • AI replies you report (Arla for Android): under any Arla reply you can tap Report this reply. You see the exact text first, and only if you agree and send is that reply text and the app version sent to api.irishwarhound.com. If your phone is offline, the report waits on the phone and is sent later. Nothing else from your conversation is included. We keep a report until we delete it, at most 13 months, read it only to review harmful or wrong AI replies, and never share or sell it.
  • Limits on forms: to stop floods, the review and waitlist forms, trial reports and problem reports use a one-way code made from your connection's address and the day; the address itself isn't stored, and the codes are deleted after two days.

Backups and crash reports

The computer keeps the newest 7 automatic profile backups and 10 manual ones. A profile backup includes records, sticky notes, the memory database, encrypted keys, gallery pictures, meetings with their recordings, and your connected email accounts' labels, calendar choices, copied events and scheduled emails (never the email sign-in itself). The phone's own backups include its scheduled emails; after a restore they wait until you approve them again. It excludes the rebuildable Library index, provider-limit notices, short-lived technical records, and error and crash logs. Restoring replaces only included records. Phone backups sent to the computer are stored separately, up to 10 per phone, and cannot be restored as desktop profiles. Backups you export remain wherever you put them. Export everything saves your records, notes, pictures and archived conversations in open formats to a folder you choose; it leaves out API keys, licence details, device-link secrets and email sign-ins. On Windows it includes your scheduled emails as stored; on Android it lists them only by their description and time.

Desktop crash reports are off by default. If you enable “Save crash reports on this computer” in Settings → Crash reports, Arla writes local files under logs\crash-<date>.jsonl. It removes detected keys and tokens, home-folder paths, email and IP addresses before writing. Each day is capped at 1 MB; files older than 14 days are pruned on the next write. Nothing uploads automatically. You choose whether to share a report.

Your choices

You can edit or delete app records, remove a paired phone, turn off memory recall, and export a backup. You can also disconnect an email account, untick calendars, and turn Sync away from home off. On Windows, Settings can open the data folder. Removing the desktop app does not itself promise to erase %APPDATA%\Arla; delete that folder separately if you want to erase its remaining records and backups. On Android, uninstalling or clearing app data removes local app storage. A cloud provider may retain requests under its own policy; contact that provider for access or deletion.

Arla asks for device permissions when a feature needs them: camera for an Inbox photo, microphone for voice and meetings, files for chosen documents and backups, network for selected services and paired devices, notifications for reminders and scheduled emails, and, on Android, alarms so a scheduled email can be sent on time (without it, it may be sent a few minutes late). Refusing a permission disables the related feature.

Arla is not directed at children under 13. Device security protects local records, and operating-system protected storage protects keys. No system is perfectly secure. We will date material policy changes and describe changes to data sharing in release notes.
